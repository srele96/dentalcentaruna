Fifty metres of cold, clean water

Ridgeway Lido has been open to swimmers since 1934. Unheated, outdoors, and open every day of the year.

Scroll to explore

Swim before work

Lanes open at 6am, and the water is usually glassy until about half past seven.

Three roped lanes run the full length: slow, medium and fast. The rest of the pool stays open for anyone who'd rather just float.

The water, month by month

No heating, so the pool follows the weather. This card is long on purpose — it scrolls fully before the next one covers it.

January to March

Four to eight degrees. Swims are short and loud. The sauna hut runs all morning and there's always someone in it telling you it's character-building.

April to June

Eleven to seventeen degrees. The nicest stretch of the year: long light, warming water, and mornings quiet enough to have a lane to yourself.

July to September

Eighteen to twenty-two degrees. Busy. Come early or come late, and bring something to sit on for the lawn.

October to December

Back down through the teens into single figures. Steam sits on the surface at dawn, which is worth the alarm on its own.

Your first cold swim

The shock is real but short. Everything below is what the lifeguards tell newcomers at the steps.

Go in slowly

Walk down the steps rather than jumping. Give your breathing thirty seconds to settle before you start swimming.

Breathe out, not in

The gasp reflex makes you inhale sharply. Long slow exhales are the fastest way to override it.

Stay where you can stand

For your first few visits, keep to the shallow end and swim across rather than along.

Get out earlier than you want to

You keep cooling for twenty minutes after you leave the water. Two minutes is a perfectly good first swim in winter.

Warm up slowly

Dry clothes and a hot drink, not a hot shower. Heating your skin too fast makes you feel worse, not better.

The sauna hut

A wood-fired cabin for twelve people, at the deep end.

Lit at 6am and kept going until close. Free with membership, £4 otherwise. Bring a towel to sit on — it's a rule, not a suggestion.

Lane etiquette

  • Pick your lane by pace, not ambition. Move down if you're being overtaken repeatedly.
  • Swim anticlockwise, keeping to the right of the rope.
  • Overtake at the wall, not mid-length.
  • Stop in the corner so the wall stays clear for turns.
  • If someone taps your foot, let them past at the next wall.

The 1934 building

Concrete, curved, and Grade II listed since 1998.

The changing village on the north side is original, down to the terrazzo floor. The filtration plant underneath it is not — that was replaced in 2019 after a two-year fundraising campaign by the members.

Events through the year

New Year's Day dip

Eleven in the morning, about four hundred people, and a queue for the sauna that lasts until lunchtime.

The Long Swim, June

Five kilometres in relay teams of four, run overnight under floodlights. Entry opens in March and fills in a week.

Midsummer late night

The pool stays open until midnight on the longest day, with the underwater lights on.

Halloween splash

Costumes in the water, which is harder than it sounds. Judged from the balcony.

Getting here

Ridgeway Lane, five minutes' walk from Ridgeway station.

Buses 12 and 44 stop at the top of the lane. Twenty parking spaces on site, free for two hours, and they're gone by eight on a summer Saturday.

The café

Porridge, toasties, and the strongest tea in the county.

Open from 6.30am. Wet swimmers welcome on the terrace, towels required on the indoor chairs.

Volunteering

The lido is run by a members' trust, and about sixty people keep it going.

  • Morning gate shifts, two hours, once a fortnight.
  • Sauna stoking, which mostly means carrying logs.
  • Poolside painting weekends each April.
  • Helping at lessons, if you hold a teaching qualification.
